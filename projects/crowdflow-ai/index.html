01 / THE QUESTION

Where does a crowd become a bottleneck?

An event entrance brings arrival patterns, screening capacity and limited queueing space into one small area. Starting with data from Lyon's Festival of Lights, CrowdFlow AI investigates where people gather, when movement slows and how an entrance layout might change those conditions.

The aim is to make a planning question tangible: which gates, barriers and paths deserve closer attention before a layout is put into use?

02 / THE METHOD

Connect observation with adjustable scenarios

CCTV trajectories and recorded contact events are processed into time-based summaries, relative observed density and directional flow maps. A social-force model then provides an adjustable simulation with controls for pedestrian count, gate width, barrier length and duration.

A Streamlit interface brings evidence, entrance design, scenario comparison and animated flow into one workflow. An optional language-model module produces interpretations of potential bottlenecks and suggestions for further exploration.

03 / WHAT EXISTS

A working environment for spatial comparison

The prototype includes data-processing scripts, a configurable simulation, an interactive dashboard and generated analysis images. Multi-gate presets explore equal-width entrances and a wider central gate through simulated throughput, queue-area density, average speed and potential contacts.

Heat maps and trajectory views help locate the spatial patterns behind those values. Together, the outputs create a way to inspect a scenario and ask more precise questions about its behavior.

04 / WHAT COMES NEXT

What makes a comparison reliable?

The current scenarios are exploratory: total entrance width and random starting conditions are not fully matched, and simulated potential contacts are not equivalent to recorded sensor events. The prototype therefore does not establish a measured improvement in crowd safety or flow.

The next step is to verify trajectory parsing, align metric definitions and compare layouts under controlled conditions. Repeated runs and parameter calibration would make the differences between layouts more interpretable.
