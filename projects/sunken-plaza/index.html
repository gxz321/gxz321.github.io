Sunken Plaza

03 / 2025

An observational study of a campus sunken plaza, using site footage, 3D reconstruction and pedestrian visualization to examine the relationship between movement, pause and public space.

01 / THE QUESTION

How do people make use of an everyday plaza?

The sunken plaza and adjacent campus shop at Tongji University's Siping Road campus accommodate passing through, resting, gathering and children's activities. Changes in level, entrances, seating and shade bring these uses into close proximity.

The group study asks three linked questions: which routes people choose, where movement and lingering concentrate, and how resting facilities and environmental conditions relate to those patterns.

02 / THE METHOD

Place observation inside a spatial model

Fixed-camera footage captures pedestrian activity, while photographs around the buildings support reconstruction in COLMAP. The geometry is scaled and aligned in Rhino, bringing the site plan, buildings and observed pedestrian positions into a common setting.

Grasshopper files and presentation footage document the making of trajectories, heat maps and animated flows. Reviewing the original video alongside the 3D views helps check how the observed activity is represented.

03 / WHAT EXISTS

A visual account of routes and pauses

The sampled footage shows people crossing the lawn, resting on steps and gathering near the shop. The study uses those observations to examine path connectivity and the relationship between entrance circulation and places to stay.

Differences in the use of seating and shade also suggest questions about comfort. Additional paths, clearer activity boundaries and more shelter are proposed design directions, with further observation needed to assess their value.

04 / WHAT COMES NEXT

Which patterns persist beyond the sample?

The findings describe exploratory observations from the recorded periods. Gaps in sampling, building detail, pedestrian projection and animation continuity limit how confidently the results can be generalized.

Further work would add continuous samples, check the alignment between observed positions and the model, and use consistent definitions for trajectories and heat maps. That would support a more reliable comparison of how the plaza is used over time.
